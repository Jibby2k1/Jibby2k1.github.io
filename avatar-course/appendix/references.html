References#

What to read first#

  • The method this course builds. Gaussian Shell Maps [Abdal et al., 2024] put Gaussians on shells around a body template. 3D Gaussian splatting [Kerbl et al., 2023] is the renderer underneath, and EWA splatting [Zwicker et al., 2001] is where its projection of a Gaussian comes from. Alpha compositing goes back to [Porter and Duff, 1984].

  • Bodies. SMPL [Loper et al., 2015] is the body model that the toy capsules stand in for; SMPL-X [Pavlakos et al., 2019], MANO [Romero et al., 2017] and FLAME [Li et al., 2017] add hands and faces. Linear blend skinning: [Magnenat-Thalmann et al., 1988]; dual quaternions as an alternative: [Kavan et al., 2007].

  • Avatars from video. People-Snapshot [Alldieck et al., 2018] set the single-camera problem; ExAvatar [Moon et al., 2024] is the baseline the project compares with. NeRF [Mildenhall et al., 2020] is the representation that splatting replaced for this use.

  • Trackers the real pipeline wires together. Body [Shen et al., 2024], masks [Ravi et al., 2025], hands [Pavlakos et al., 2024], faces [Retsinas et al., 2024].

  • Evaluation. Metrics: [Wang et al., 2004], [Zhang et al., 2018]. Pre-registration: [Nosek et al., 2018]. The bootstrap: [Efron and Tibshirani, 1994]. Geometry of several cameras: [Hartley and Zisserman, 2004]; calibration: [Zhang, 2000].

  • Motion and numerics. Body-segment masses: [Winter, 2009]; soft-tissue motion: [Pons-Moll et al., 2015]; the optimiser: [Kingma and Ba, 2015]; floating point: [Goldberg, 1991].

The repository’s own reference list, with notes on how each work is used, is docs/REFERENCES.md.
