Notation and conventions#

One table of symbols for the whole course. A lesson that needs a symbol for something else says so where it does. The code names are those of course_utils (imported as cu).

Coordinate conventions#

Frame

Convention

In code

World

Right-handed, metres. \(y\) points up, the floor is \(y = 0\). The person faces \(+z\) and the person’s left is \(+x\).

cu.REST_JOINTS, every trans

Camera

The OpenCV convention: \(x\) right, \(y\) down, \(z\) forward into the scene. \(X_c = W X + t\).

cam.R is \(W\), cam.T is \(t\)

Image

Pixels, origin at the top-left corner, \(u\) to the right and \(v\) down. \(u = f_x\, x / z + c_x\), \(v = f_y\, y / z + c_y\). Pixel centres are at integer plus one half.

cam.project, cu.pixel_grid

Surface

\((u, v) \in [0, 1)^2\) on each body part. \(u\) runs around the limb (\(u = 0\) faces \(+z\) on upright parts) and wraps; \(v\) runs along it from pole to pole.

cu.capsule_surface

Rotations

A joint rotation is an axis-angle vector: its direction is the axis and its length the angle in radians. A Gaussian’s rotation is a quaternion \((w, x, y, z)\).

cu.axis_angle_to_matrix, cu.quat_to_rotmat

Orbit cameras

Azimuth 0 looks at the person’s front, from \(+z\). Positive azimuth moves the camera toward the person’s left (\(+x\)).

cu.orbit_camera

Images

Height \(\times\) width \(\times\) 3, values in \([0, 1]\). Row 0 is the top.

cu.show_images

The rest pose is a T-pose. The same letter can mean two things across parts of the course; the table lists both and the part where each applies. The two to watch are \(T\) (transmittance in Part 2, texture from Part 4 on) and \(u, v\) (pixel coordinates of a camera, surface coordinates of a body part).

Symbols#

Gaussians and splatting (Part 2)#

Symbol

Meaning

In code

\(\mu\)

centre (mean) of a Gaussian, in 3D or, after projection, \(\mu'\) in pixels

means, mean2d

\(\Sigma\), \(\Sigma'\)

covariance in 3D (3 × 3) and on the image (2 × 2)

cov3d, cov2d

\(s = (s_1, s_2, s_3)\), \(S\)

axis lengths (scale) and \(S = \operatorname{diag}(s)\)

scales

\(R\)

rotation of a Gaussian (from a quaternion \(q\)); \(\Sigma = R S S^\top R^\top\)

rot

\(o\)

opacity of a Gaussian, in \((0, 1)\)

opacity

\(c_i\)

colour of Gaussian \(i\)

colors

\(G_i(x)\)

the unnormalised Gaussian \(\exp(-\tfrac12 d^\top \Sigma^{-1} d)\) at pixel \(x\), \(d = x - \mu\)

cu.gaussian_2d

\(\alpha_i\)

alpha of Gaussian \(i\) at a pixel, \(\alpha_i = o_i\, G_i(x)\)

\(T_i\)

transmittance in front of Gaussian \(i\), \(T_i = \prod_{j<i} (1 - \alpha_j)\)

cu.composite_dense

\(w_i\)

compositing weight, \(w_i = \alpha_i T_i\)

\(C(x)\), \(c_\text{bg}\)

pixel colour \(\sum_i c_i w_i + T_{N+1} c_\text{bg}\), and the background colour (\(b\) in Part 5)

bg

\(W\), \(t\)

world-to-camera rotation and translation

cam.R, cam.T

\(f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y\)

focal lengths and principal point, in pixels (the intrinsics, often collected in a matrix \(K\))

cam.fx, …

\(\pi\)

the pinhole projection, camera coordinates to pixels

cam.project

\(J\)

Jacobian of \(\pi\) at the Gaussian’s centre (2 × 3); \(\Sigma' = J W \Sigma W^\top J^\top\)

cu.project_gaussians

\(H \times W\)

image height and width in pixels (context separates this \(W\) from the rotation)

cam.height, cam.width

Body, pose and skinning (Part 3)#

Symbol

Meaning

In code

\(j\), \(p(j)\)

a joint and its parent; there are 17 joints

cu.JOINTS, cu.PARENTS

\(r_j\)

rest position of joint \(j\)

cu.REST_JOINTS

\(\theta\), \(\theta_t\)

a pose: one axis-angle rotation per joint; the pose of frame \(t\)

poses (frames, joints, 3)

\(\tau\)

translation of the whole body

trans

\(R_j\)

rotation of joint \(j\) relative to its parent, from Rodrigues’ formula

cu.axis_angle_to_matrix

\(G_j\)

world transform of joint \(j\) (4 × 4), \(G_j = G_{p(j)} L_j\)

cu.forward_kinematics

\(A_j\)

skinning transform of joint \(j\), \(A_j = G_j (G_j^\text{rest})^{-1}\)

cu.skinning_transforms

\(w_{ij}\)

skinning weight of point \(i\) for joint \(j\); \(\sum_j w_{ij} = 1\)

cu.skin_weights

\(M_i\)

blended transform of point \(i\), \(M_i = \sum_j w_{ij} A_j\); then \(\mu' = M\mu + t\) and \(\Sigma' = M \Sigma M^\top\)

cu.lbs

Shells and texture (Part 4)#

Symbol

Meaning

In code

\((u, v)\)

surface coordinates on a body part

avatar.u, avatar.v

\(S(u, v)\), \(n(u, v)\)

template surface point and its unit normal

cu.capsule_surface

\(d_k\), \(S_k\)

offset of shell \(k\) along the normal, and the shell \(S_k = S + d_k\, n\)

avatar.offsets

\(F\)

local frame at a surface point: columns are the tangents along \(u\) and \(v\) and the normal

avatar.rest_frame

\(T\), \(T_k\)

the texture: per shell and part, an image with 7 channels (3 colour, 1 opacity, 3 size)

avatar.texture

\(\sigma(\cdot)\)

the logistic function; \(c = \sigma(T_{0:3})\), \(o = \sigma(T_3)\), \(s = s_0 \odot \exp(T_{4:7})\)

avatar.gaussians

\(s_0\)

base size of a Gaussian, tied to the spacing between samples

avatar.spacing

Symbol

Meaning

\(c_f(t)\)

Evaluation (Part 8)#

Symbol

Meaning

In code

MSE, PSNR

mean squared error over pixels, and \(\text{PSNR} = -10 \log_{10} \text{MSE}\) in dB for images in \([0, 1]\)

cu.psnr

fg, bw

PSNR inside the true mask only; whole-frame PSNR averaged over a black and a white background

cu.evaluate, ev.evaluate_bw

IoU

intersection over union of the rendered silhouette and the mask

cu.evaluate

\(m_s\)

a metric of the run with seed \(s\)

spread

seed spread, \(\max_s m_s - \min_s m_s\); with two seeds, \(\lvert m_0 - m_1 \rvert\)

ev.seed_spread

counted

a difference with the same sign on every seed, above the spread and above a registered floor

ev.counted

A, B, C

cameras of a blind-camera study: A and B may be used for fitting, C only by the scorer

A/A, AB/A, AB/AB

conditions, written “enrol with / drive with”

px

pixels, the unit of landmark error in the blind camera

ev.landmark_error

Units#

Lengths are in metres, angles in radians inside the code and in degrees in prose and plots, time in seconds, image error in dB (PSNR) or pixels (landmarks). Colours and opacities are in \([0, 1]\).
