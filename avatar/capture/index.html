Avatar · Recording guide

How to film someone for a 3‑D avatar, with one camera

This is the protocol for the next recording behind my avatar. It needs one phone, a helper, a printed checkerboard and a tape measure. The camera can move freely between takes, because what the software needs to know about it belongs to the lens, not to where it stands.

Camera
1, moved between takes
People
Subject + helper
Takes
8, in 13 short files
Time
About 40 min

Before you start

Equipment

  • One phone or camera, on its main 1× lens.
  • A tripod, or any stable mount, for the movement takes.
  • A helper for the handheld orbits.
  • A checkerboard: print the A3 board (30 mm squares) or, if A3 is not possible, the A4 board (22 mm squares) at 100 % / “Actual size”, never “Fit to page”. Glue it flat to stiff card or foam board, keep the white border, and measure one square to 0.5 mm.
  • Floor tape and a tape measure.

The room

  • Even, diffuse light from several sides. Avoid a window whose light changes and hard shadows, and keep the helper’s shadow off the subject during the orbits.
  • The whole room is background, because the orbit films every direction. No mirrors, TV screens or reflections, and nobody else in frame: the person detector must only ever see the subject. Walls that contrast with the clothes help.
  • Floor marks: a tape cross where the subject stands, and two foot marks hip-width apart, so every take starts from the same spot.
Camera settings: lock every one before take 0 and never change it mid-session

Do not close the camera app or tap the screen between takes. If a lock is lost, re-lock it and write that down.

Copy the original files off the phone by cable or file copy, not through a messaging app, which re-compresses the video. On an iPhone, first set Settings › Photos › Transfer to Mac or PC to “Keep Originals”.

Stop recording between passes and before moving the camera or the tripod, so each pass or position is its own file. Name the files as in the Files column.

Take 0Helper or tripod · 45–60 s

Calibrate the lens

  • The camera stays still, on the tripod or in the helper’s hands, with the exact recording settings and locks. The subject holds the board about 2–2.5 m away and moves it.
  • Move it slowly through the whole frame: the centre, all four corners and the edges. Spend most of the time on the corners.
  • Tilt it by as much as 45° each way: left, right, up and down.
  • Skip the corners and the lens model there is a guess. The calibration tool warns when one was missed.

Take 1Helper, handheld · 3 passes

Static orbit in an A-pose

  • The subject stands on the foot marks, arms straight about 45° from the body, palms forward, chin level, eyes on a fixed point, and holds as still as is comfortable, breathing normally.
  • The helper walks one full, slow circle per pass at a constant 2–2.5 m, keeping the whole body in frame and roughly centred, at the same camera height all the way round.
  • Pass 1: camera at chest height, level. Pass 2: held high above the helper’s own head, tilted down, to see the top of the head, the shoulders and the tops of the feet. Pass 3: at knee height, tilted up, to see under the chin and arms and the inner legs.
  • Stop recording after each pass. Rest, then return to the marks and the same pose.

Why a steady distance and height: the tracker treats an orbit like the subject turning in place and holds the body near where it stood in the first frame. Keeping the body centred, at a constant distance and height, keeps that true.

Take 2Helper, handheld · 2 passes

Static orbit, arms up

  • Arms raised overhead in a wide V, palms forward.
  • One pass at chest height, one low and tilted up.
  • This take is meant to remove the grey speckle under the arms.

Take 3Tripod, front · 3 slow turns

Turn in place

  • The tripod close enough that the body fills most of the frame height, with about 20 cm to spare above the head and below the feet (about 2.5–3 m in portrait). Lens at waist-to-chest height, floor visible.
  • Two slow turns on the cross with the arms out, then one with the arms raised in a wide V.

Take 4Tripod, two positions · ~20 s per move

Movement library

Same framing as take 3. Each move once slowly, then once at natural speed:

  • wave with both hands
  • squats
  • jumping jacks
  • march in place
  • arms raised up and back down
  • arm circles
  • side bends
  • torso twists
  • a few steps forward and back

Then stop recording, move the tripod 60–90° around the subject, and do the library again as a new file. That is not a second camera, because the two views are not simultaneous, but it gives the model a second viewpoint of every move.

Take 5Tripod, its own position · 60 s

The sealed test

  • Mixed movement, including at least one move not in take 4: lunges, reaching to the floor, or a slow spin.
  • Filmed from a tripod position no other take uses, for example 45°.
  • Name the file sealed, do not open or share it, and keep it apart from the rest.
  • It is scored once, after every choice about the model is final. That is what makes a later quality claim honest.

Take 6As take 0 · 45 s

Calibrate again

The same as take 0. If the two calibrations disagree, a setting slipped during the session.

Take 7Helper, close · 60 s + 45 s

Face and hands (optional, last)

  • A slow arc about 1 m from the head: a neutral expression, then a smile.
  • Each hand held up, front and back, fingers spread, then closed.
  • This distance needs refocusing, which slightly changes the lens model. Lock focus on the face, and before unlocking, film another board clip (7-board) at about 1 m.

What to wear

  • Fitted, lightly textured clothes, such as a fitted long-sleeve top and leggings. Avoid all black, which hides both detail and shape, and loose fabric, which moves in ways a body model cannot represent.
  • The same outfit, hair and shoes in every take. Barefoot, or the shoes the avatar should wear.
  • Hair tied back, or the same way throughout.
  • Nothing reflective: no watch, jewellery or glossy logos.

Body measurements

Lengths matter most: they fix where the joints are, which the fit otherwise has to infer. Stand relaxed, use a mirror or the helper to find the landmarks, and record both readings.

Also take one front and one side photo on the tape cross, in the outfit.

Afterwards

What to hand over

  • The original video files, named as in the takes table.
  • The board’s measured square size.
  • A short notes.txt: the camera model, app and settings, the take order, where the tripod stood for takes 3–5, any lock that was lost, and the measurements.

All of it stays on one local machine. Personal video and measurements are never uploaded or committed anywhere.

What is ready, and what is not

  • Ready, proven on real footage: the single-camera pipeline that built the current avatar from a turn in place and a short movement clip, with a guessed lens.
  • Built, not yet run on real footage: the checkerboard calibration and the step that prepares frames with the real lens. On simulated recordings the calibration finds the focal length to within 0.15 %, but it meets its accuracy target across the whole image only when the board reaches all four corners, and its strictest test, at the very corners, failed; that is recorded, not hidden. Take 0 is its first real test.
  • Not built yet: a fit made for the orbit, with the body fixed and the camera moving, and the scoring of this session’s sealed take. Until the orbit fit exists, the orbit is tracked as a turn in place, which has not yet been tried on an orbit.
  • No helper? A tripod with takes 0 and 3–6 still works: the earlier method, now with a real lens model, fitted clothes, the arms-up turn and full measurements.
